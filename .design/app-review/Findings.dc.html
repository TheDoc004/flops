Review findings

A pass over all 14 routes and all 5 persistent chrome surfaces. Ordered by how much each one shapes the product, not by how fast it can be fixed. Four are still open calls; three shipped in 640c769 and one is settled.

Correction to the first version of this canvas

It claimed Training had no page title. It does — <h1 class="page-title">Training</h1> lives in app/layouts/TrainingLayout.jsx, the layout shell. The earlier grep was scoped to features/training-workouts/ and missed it. The per-page <h2 class="section-title"> is a correct sub-heading under that h1. That finding is withdrawn.

01

Two sub-navs, two active-state metaphorsOpen

LibrarySubNav slides a white thumb along a grey track with --color-primary type. .plan-tab.is-active fills the pill with --color-text-strong and inverts. Both are pill-shaped; neither resembles the other in behaviour.

Why it matters — Recipes → Plan is two clicks, and the answer to “which tab am I on?” changes on the way. This is the clearest chrome inconsistency in the app.

Pick one. The sliding thumb is the more distinctive of the two and already handles a two-item set; plan-tabs would need it to handle three.

02

The nav item for the plan sectionSettled

It read Goals → /plan, colliding with the Goals tab inside PlanLayout — one word meaning both the section and one of its three tabs.

Why it matters — Report and Profile sat under a nav item that never mentioned them.

Settled: Profile → /plan/profile, with matchPaths: ['/plan'] so it stays lit across the section. This canvas originally argued for “Plan”; that was proposed, briefly shipped in 640c769, and reverted in 1bd9da2. Profile is the intended label — the Goals collision is resolved either way.

03

Two styling systemsOpen

Nutrition styles through global classes in index.css. Training and the shared navs use CSS modules. Both consume the same tokens, so nothing looks broken.

Why it matters — A fork with no signpost: every future shared control has to pick a side, and today the answer is “whichever domain builds it first”.

A deliberate call, not a cleanup. Worth deciding before the Phase 3 bridge adds UI that belongs to both domains.

04

Auth and onboarding sit outside the design systemOpen

Login.jsx and Onboarding.jsx use bare <h1> with their own modules — no .page-title, no DM Serif. Onboarding is also the only surface spelling the brand FLOPS; the boot screen, Login, Landing and Navbar all say Flops.

Why it matters — These are the first two screens a new person sees, and they do not look like the product behind them.

Adopt .page-title on both, and settle the wordmark on one casing.

05

--color-text-faint carries the most sub-AA textApplied

4.26:1 off-today, across .section-label (11px), .empty-state (15px) and gym .prev (12px) — 64 call sites.

Why it matters — The smallest type in the app has the least contrast, and it is worst on notebook days.

Applied in 640c769: #a1a1aa → #a8a8b0. 4.63:1 on the card, 5.94:1 on the page.

06

--color-success at 12pxApplied

#059669 on cream is 3.58:1 — the lowest pair in the palette, in .macro-status.is-ok.

Why it matters — The “on target” indicator you read every day is the least legible text on the Dashboard.

Applied in 640c769: #059669 → #047a55 — not the #04815a first proposed here. Success text also renders directly on --color-bg (the paste confirmation in Dashboard.jsx), and cream is a harder background than the card: #04815a reached only 4.20:1 there. #047a55 clears both (4.60 / 5.09).

07
